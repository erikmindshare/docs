---
title: "Quickstart"
description: "Build, publish and answer a real survey in about fifteen minutes — starting from a template rather than a blank sheet."
---


The fastest way to understand AutoSurvey is to publish something and answer it yourself. This takes
about fifteen minutes and ends with a working survey you can send to someone.

<Note>
  You need AutoSurvey installed and setup finished first. If the **Setup Home** page shows
  outstanding checks, clear those before starting — see [Setup Home](/admin/setup).
</Note>

## 1. Create a suite

Open the **Survey Suites** tab and choose **New Survey**.

A *suite* is the container: it holds your questions, its own Google Sheet, its releases, and the
responses that come back. One suite per survey programme is the usual shape.

## 2. Start from a template

You could start from an empty sheet — but the quickest useful survey is a template, and four ship
with the product:

| Template | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer Satisfaction (CSAT)** | How happy customers are after a purchase or support interaction, and what drives it |
| **Net Promoter Score (NPS)** | The 0–10 recommend question, the reason behind it, and a follow-up opt-in |
| **Event Feedback** | Overall rating, what worked across sessions, venue and networking, and what people want next |
| **New Customer Onboarding** | Friction in the first weeks — how easy setup was, and where people are stuck |

Pick one. Its questions and choice lists are written into your suite's Google Sheet.

<Tip>
  Two **demos** also ship as complete, runnable surveys — no building required. **Display-Type
  Gallery** has one question of every display type, which is the fastest way to see what each
  control actually looks like. **Customer Satisfaction (demo)** is a complete CSAT survey. Demos
  start with no responses, so their reports open empty — the fast path is to open the demo in the
  runner, submit one test response yourself, then open the reports.
</Tip>

## 3. Look at it, and change one thing

Open the suite's Google Sheet. Every question is a row on the **Questions** tab.

Change something small so you can see the loop work — reword a question, or set one to
`Required = TRUE`. Then open the **AutoSurvey** sidebar (the **AutoSurvey** menu) and choose
**Run Validation**.

<Note>
  Validation checks your workbook. The stricter check happens at import, and the two look at
  different things — so a clean sidebar isn't a guarantee. That's normal; import is the real test.
  See [Troubleshooting](/support/troubleshooting).
</Note>

## 4. Import

Import the suite into Salesforce.

Importing never makes anything live. Your changes land as **Unpublished changes** and wait for you,
which means you can import as often as you like while you're working.

## 5. Publish

Open the **Publish & Share** panel.

Before you commit, it shows what will change — in particular how many reporting fields this publish
will add to your Response Object. On a first publish that number is roughly your question count,
because the object is being built.

Click **Release suite** (on a later version the button reads **Release v2**, **Release v3**, and
so on). A dialog — *Review changes before releasing* — gives you a last look; click **Release**.
The survey is live immediately, and its public link goes live with it.

<Note>
  Reporting fields are provisioned in the background just after go-live. If you see *"Reporting
  setup didn’t start — your release is live, but responses may not appear in reports yet. Use
  “Review & repair reporting” from Get responses to retry."*, the survey is still live and still
  capturing every response — run **Review & repair reporting** from **Get responses** when
  convenient. Responses are never at risk while that runs.
</Note>

## 6. Answer your own survey

In **Publish & Share**, the **Public link** row of the **Channels** band now shows the live link,
a **Copy link** button, and a QR code (under **QR ▾**).

<Warning>
  **Open it in a private window.** You're signed in to Salesforce, so the link will work for you
  whether or not public access is set up correctly. A private window tests what a respondent
  actually gets — and this is the single most common way a launch goes wrong.
</Warning>

Fill it in and submit.

## 7. Find the response

Open your Response Object. There's one record for the survey you just completed, with a field per
question — ordinary Salesforce data you can report on, chart, and automate against.

That's the whole loop: **write → validate → import → publish → collect**.
