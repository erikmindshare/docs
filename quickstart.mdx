The fastest way to understand AutoSurvey is to answer a survey yourself and then build your own.
If you have an AutoSurvey trial, your org is already set up and it already holds a sample survey.
This page starts there. It takes about fifteen minutes.

<Note>
  **No trial?** If you installed AutoSurvey in your own org, finish **Setup Home** first and then go
  to [Build a survey in your own org](#build-a-survey-in-your-own-org) below.
</Note>

## 1. Open your login email

When your trial org is ready, Salesforce sends you an email with a link to set your password. The
email comes from Salesforce, not from AutoSurvey. Nobody else knows the password that you set.

Click the link and choose a password. After that, you log in on the Salesforce login page with the
username that came with your trial.

<Frame caption="You log in on the Salesforce login page. The first time, Salesforce can ask for a one-time email code or ask you to set up an authenticator app. That is normal and happens once.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/01-log-in.png" alt="The Salesforce login page, with a Username field and a Log In button." />
</Frame>

If you do not see the AutoSurvey tabs after you log in, open the App Launcher (the grid of dots at
the top left) and choose **AutoSurvey**.

## 2. Open the sample survey

Click the **Surveys** tab. Your trial org already holds two surveys:

- **Customer Satisfaction (demo)** is released. It is ready to answer.
- **Coffee CSAT (tone demo)** is a draft.

Click **Customer Satisfaction (demo)** in the list on the left.

<Frame caption="The Surveys tab of a trial org. The two sample surveys are already in the list.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/02-seeded-survey.png" alt="The AutoSurvey Surveys tab with Coffee CSAT (tone demo) and Customer Satisfaction (demo) in the survey list, 2 surveys below the list, and a Select a survey panel on the right." />
</Frame>

The survey opens with its name, **v1 · Accepting responses**, and **Public link active** in the
header. Below the header are five tabs: **Questions**, **Design**, **Send**, **Results** and
**Settings**.

## 3. Preview it and find its link

Open the **Questions** tab and click **Preview**. You see each screen as a respondent sees it. Use
the **Screen** numbers to move between screens.

The Questions tab also shows this banner: "This survey is not bound to a Google Sheet, so this
panel cannot edit its questions." The sample has no sheet, so you cannot edit its questions here.
To edit questions, build your own survey (step 6).

<Frame caption="Questions tab, Preview. This is what a respondent sees.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/03-preview.png" alt="The Questions tab in Preview, showing screen 1 of 4 of Customer Satisfaction (demo) with five answer choices." />
</Frame>

Then open the **Send** tab and click **All channels**. The **Public link** row shows **ACTIVE**.
Anyone with this link can answer, with no sign-in. Click **Copy link** to copy it, or open **QR ▾**
for a QR code.

<Frame caption="Send tab, All channels. The public link is active as soon as the survey is released.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/03-share-link.png" alt="The Send tab with All channels selected. The Public link row shows ACTIVE with Copy link and QR buttons. In-app, Site members and Email rows are below it." />
</Frame>

<Note>
  In a trial org, **Email** shows **NOT SET UP**. That does not stop the public link. Send email
  invitations later, after you set up email.
</Note>

## 4. Answer it

Click **Try it** in the survey header. It offers two ways:

- **As yourself** runs the survey inside Salesforce.
- **As a guest on your site** opens the public page that your respondents see.

Test runs from **Try it** are not counted in results.

<Frame caption="Try it runs the survey without adding a response to your results.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/04-try-it.png" alt="The Try it menu open, with As yourself and As a guest on your site." />
</Frame>

To add a real response, paste the public link from step 3 into a **private browser window** and
answer the survey there. You are signed in to Salesforce in your normal window, so a private window
is the only way to see exactly what a respondent sees.

<Frame caption="The public survey page, opened from the public link with no sign-in.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/04-public-page.png" alt="The public page of Customer Satisfaction (demo), showing the first question, Overall, how satisfied are you with us?, with five choices and a Next button." />
</Frame>

## 5. Find the response

Open the survey's **Results** tab. The **RESPONSES** panel counts finished responses. Click
**Refresh** after you submit. Before anyone answers, it says that no responses are there yet.

<Frame caption="The Results tab before the first response. A response shows here when someone finishes the survey.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/05-results.png" alt="The Results tab with a RESPONSES panel that reads Accepting responses, 0 responses, and No responses yet." />
</Frame>

The **Responses** tab of the app lists every response from every survey. AutoSurvey also copies
each response to the survey's reporting object, with a field for each question. That is ordinary
Salesforce data: you can report on it, chart it and automate against it.

## 6. Build your own survey

Go back to the **Surveys** tab and click **New Survey**. If AutoSurvey asks
**Create in which workspace?**, pick a workspace and click **Continue**. A workspace is a folder for
surveys. It does not change who can answer a survey.

**Choose how to start** offers six ways. The fastest is **Start from a sample**. The samples are in
groups by topic, for example *Customer experience* and *Onboarding and adoption*. Type in
**Filter samples** to find one. Open a sample to see **What it asks**, then click
**Use this sample**.

<Frame caption="New Survey, Start from a sample. Open a sample to see what it asks.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/06-start-from-a-sample.png" alt="The New survey dialog on Choose how to start, with Start from a sample selected and the Net Promoter Score (NPS) sample open." />
</Frame>

**Review your survey** shows the questions before anything is created. Check the **Title**, the
**Workspace** and the **Survey ID**, then click **Create survey**.

<Frame caption="Review your survey. Nothing is created until you click Create survey.">
  <img src="/images/quickstart/06-review.png" alt="The Review your survey screen with Title, Workspace and Survey ID fields, the questions listed by screen, and a Create survey button." />
</Frame>

To go on from here — change a question, release the survey, and send it — follow
[Build a survey in your own org](#build-a-survey-in-your-own-org), from
[Review and create](#review-and-create).

## Build a survey in your own org

This is the full loop for an org where you installed AutoSurvey yourself: **start → edit → try →
release → send → collect**.

<Note>
  You need AutoSurvey installed and setup finished first. If the **Setup Home** page shows
  outstanding checks, clear those before starting — see [Setup Home](/admin/setup).
</Note>

### Start a new survey

Open the **Surveys** tab and click **New Survey**.

If AutoSurvey asks **Create in which workspace?**, pick a workspace and click **Continue**. A
workspace is a folder for surveys. It does not change who can answer a survey.

<Note>
  If an admin turned on **Show survey suites** on Setup Home, the app says *suite* where this guide
  says *survey* — for example **New Suite** and **Create suite**. A suite is a survey with more than
  one form. Most orgs never need it.
</Note>

### Start from a sample

On **Choose how to start**, select **Start from a sample**. The sample library groups its surveys
by topic, for example *Customer experience* and *Onboarding and adoption*, and you can type in
**Filter samples** to find one. Open a sample to see **What it asks**, then click
**Use this sample**.

<Tip>
  Want to look before you build? On the empty Surveys page, **Explore a sample survey** opens
  **Example surveys**. **Try a ready-made sample** creates a complete survey you can open and run
  straight away. **Display-Type Gallery** has one question of every display type — the fastest way
  to see what each control looks like. **Customer Satisfaction (demo)** is a complete CSAT survey.
  Demos start with no responses, so their results open empty.
</Tip>

### Review and create

The **Review** step shows the questions before anything is created. Switch between **List** and
**Preview** to read them as a list or as the respondent sees them.

Check three fields at the top:

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Title** | The survey name. You can rename it later. |
| **Workspace** | Where the survey goes. You can move it later. |
| **Survey ID** | Part of the public link — the hint shows how your link ends. It cannot change after you create the survey. |

Below them, **Look** lets you pick a theme and a palette. It is optional, and you can change it any
time on the **Design** tab. **Advanced** holds the description and where answers are stored; both
have defaults.

Click **Create survey**. The survey opens on its **Questions** tab, in **Preview**.

### Change one thing, and try it

Change something small so you can see the loop work. In **Preview**, click the pencil on a question
to open its editor. Reword the question, or turn on **Required**, then click **Close**. The status
line reads **Draft saved · Release to publish**.

Click **Try it** in the survey header to run the survey yourself. If your org has a public site,
**Try it** offers **As yourself** and **As a guest on your site**. Test runs are not counted in
results.

<Note>
  You edit questions in the app. Google Sheet sync is an advanced option for bulk edits — the
  **Sheet sync** button in the header opens it. See [Sheets to Flow](/workflows/sheets-to-flow).
</Note>

### Release

Nothing you change is live until you release it. In the survey header, click **Release v1**. On a
later version the button counts your changes, for example **Release 3 changes**.

The **Release version 1** dialog shows what changes for respondents, in the survey, and in
Salesforce — including the reporting fields this release adds. Click **Release version 1**. The
survey is live immediately, and its public link goes live with it. The dialog then shows
**Version 1 is released**, with **Go to Send** to share it.

<Note>
  Reporting setup runs in the background just after go-live. If the dialog says *Reporting setup
  failed. Version 1 is released.*, the survey is still live and AutoSurvey still saves every
  response. Click **Repair reporting** when convenient.
</Note>

### Answer your own survey

Open the **Send** tab and click **All channels**. The **Public link** row has a **Copy link** button and a QR
code (under **QR ▾**). If the row says the link needs setup, click **Set up…** and follow the steps.

<Warning>
  **Open it in a private window.** You're signed in to Salesforce, so the link will work for you
  whether or not public access is set up correctly. A private window tests what a respondent
  actually gets — and this is the single most common way a launch goes wrong.
</Warning>

Fill it in and submit.

### Find your response

Open the survey's **Results** tab. Your response is there. The **Responses** tab in the app lists
every response, and AutoSurvey copies each one to the survey's reporting object, with a field per
question — ordinary Salesforce data you can report on, chart, and automate against.

That's the whole loop: **start → edit → try → release → send → collect**.
