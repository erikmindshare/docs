---
title: "Quickstart"
description: "Build, release and answer a real survey in about fifteen minutes — starting from a sample rather than a blank page."
---


The fastest way to understand AutoSurvey is to release something and answer it yourself. This takes
about fifteen minutes and ends with a working survey you can send to someone.

<Note>
  You need AutoSurvey installed and setup finished first. If the **Setup Home** page shows
  outstanding checks, clear those before starting — see [Setup Home](/admin/setup).
</Note>

## 1. Start a new survey

Open the **Surveys** tab and click **New Survey**.

If AutoSurvey asks **Create in which workspace?**, pick a workspace and click **Continue**. A
workspace is a folder for surveys. It does not change who can answer a survey.

<Note>
  If an admin turned on **Show survey suites** on Setup Home, the app says *suite* where this guide
  says *survey* — for example **New Suite** and **Create suite**. A suite is a survey with more than
  one form. Most orgs never need it.
</Note>

## 2. Start from a sample

On **Choose how to start**, select **Start from a sample**. The sample library groups its surveys
by topic, for example *Customer experience* and *Onboarding and adoption*, and you can type in
**Filter samples** to find one. Open a sample to see **What it asks**, then click
**Use this sample**.

<Tip>
  Want to look before you build? On the empty Surveys page, **Explore a sample survey** opens
  **Example surveys**. **Try a ready-made sample** creates a complete survey you can open and run
  straight away. **Display-Type Gallery** has one question of every display type — the fastest way
  to see what each control looks like. **Customer Satisfaction (demo)** is a complete CSAT survey.
  Demos start with no responses, so their results open empty.
</Tip>

## 3. Review and create

The **Review** step shows the questions before anything is created. Switch between **List** and
**Preview** to read them as a list or as the respondent sees them.

Check three fields at the top:

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Title** | The survey name. You can rename it later. |
| **Workspace** | Where the survey goes. You can move it later. |
| **Survey ID** | Part of the public link — the hint shows how your link ends. It cannot change after you create the survey. |

Below them, **Look** lets you pick a theme and a palette. It is optional, and you can change it any
time on the **Design** tab. **Advanced** holds the description and where answers are stored; both
have defaults.

Click **Create survey**. The survey opens on its **Questions** tab, in **Preview**.

## 4. Change one thing, and try it

Change something small so you can see the loop work. In **Preview**, click the pencil on a question
to open its editor. Reword the question, or turn on **Required**, then click **Close**. The status
line reads **Draft saved · Release to publish**.

Click **Try it** in the survey header to run the survey yourself. If your org has a public site,
**Try it** offers **As yourself** and **As a guest on your site**. Test runs are not counted in
results.

<Note>
  You edit questions in the app. Google Sheet sync is an advanced option for bulk edits — the
  **Sheet sync** button in the header opens it. See [Sheets to Flow](/workflows/sheets-to-flow).
</Note>

## 5. Release

Nothing you change is live until you release it. In the survey header, click **Release v1**. On a
later version the button counts your changes, for example **Release 3 changes**.

The **Release version 1** dialog shows what changes for respondents, in the survey, and in
Salesforce — including the reporting fields this release adds. Click **Release version 1**. The
survey is live immediately, and its public link goes live with it. The dialog then shows
**Version 1 is released**, with **Go to Send** to share it.

<Note>
  Reporting setup runs in the background just after go-live. If the dialog says *Reporting setup
  failed. Version 1 is released.*, the survey is still live and AutoSurvey still saves every
  response. Click **Repair reporting** when convenient.
</Note>

## 6. Answer your own survey

Open the **Send** tab and click **All channels**. The **Public link** row has a **Copy link** button and a QR
code (under **QR ▾**). If the row says the link needs setup, click **Set up…** and follow the steps.

<Warning>
  **Open it in a private window.** You're signed in to Salesforce, so the link will work for you
  whether or not public access is set up correctly. A private window tests what a respondent
  actually gets — and this is the single most common way a launch goes wrong.
</Warning>

Fill it in and submit.

## 7. Find the response

Open the survey's **Results** tab. Your response is there. The **Responses** tab in the app lists
every response, and AutoSurvey copies each one to the survey's reporting object, with a field per
question — ordinary Salesforce data you can report on, chart, and automate against.

That's the whole loop: **start → edit → try → release → send → collect**.
