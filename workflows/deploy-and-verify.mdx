---
title: "Publish & Share"
description: "What publishing actually does, why your survey goes live before reporting setup finishes, and how to share the link with people who have no Salesforce login."
---


Importing a survey never makes it live. **Releasing** does — and it takes effect immediately.

Open your suite from the **Survey Suites** tab and go to the **Publish & Share** panel.

## Step 1 — See what will change

The panel shows three things before you commit to anything:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Current release** | What's live now — its label, when it published, and who published it. Blank if nothing has gone live yet |
| **Unpublished changes** | Appears when anything is waiting to go live — a new import, settings-only changes, or sheet edits you haven't re-imported yet |
| **Reporting-field summary** | How many new fields this release will add to your Response Object |

<Tip>
  If the new-field count looks higher than you expect, check the Questions tab for accidental extra
  rows before you release — each question row mints exactly one reporting field, so stray rows
  inflate the count. That number is the cheapest early warning the panel gives you.
</Tip>

## Step 2 — The Response Object (first release only)

Responses are stored in a Salesforce custom object — the **Response Object**. Each completed survey
creates a record, and each question gets a reporting field on it.

| What the summary says | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The object doesn't exist | It's created by your first release |
| The object exists, all fields present | Nothing changes |
| The object exists, N fields missing | This release adds N fields |

<Warning>
  **The Response Object is locked after the first release**, so responses already collected can't be
  orphaned by a later change. Decide where responses should live before that first release, not
  after.
</Warning>

## Step 3 — Release

Review the summary and click **Release suite** — the button reads **Release v2**, **Release v3** and
so on when a staged version is waiting. A **Review changes before releasing** dialog recaps exactly
what this release will do; its **Release** button mints the new release, which becomes live
immediately.

<Note>
  **Going live never waits on reporting setup.** The release is live the moment you click **Release**;
  provisioning the new reporting fields happens in the background afterwards, usually within a
  minute. Respondents can start answering straight away.
</Note>

### If reporting setup needs attention

Occasionally the background field setup doesn't finish first time. When that happens the panel
warns: *"Reporting setup didn’t start — your release is live, but responses may not appear in
reports yet. Use “Review & repair reporting” from Get responses to retry."* A banner also stays on
the release panel until it's fixed, with the full reason and fix guidance for known failures.

The important part: **the survey stays live and every response is still captured.** Reporting-field
state does not affect whether answers are saved — it affects where they show up for reporting.
**Repair reporting…** (which opens the **Review & repair reporting** dialog) is safe to run as many
times as you like; it re-checks what's needed and starts provisioning again.

## Step 4 — Share it

Once a release is live, the **Public link** row of the **Get responses** band gives you the link: a
**Copy link** button, and a **QR ▾** menu with an on-screen scannable preview plus **Download PNG**
and **Copy image** for print or in-person use. The confirmation you see right after releasing offers
**Copy public link** too.

### If respondents don't have a Salesforce login

Most surveys go to people outside your org. The **Public link** row's readiness checks cover
everything a public link needs — an Experience site bound, the runner page reachable on it, guest
access granted, and this survey serving a live release — and tell you which piece isn't ready.
**Public link setup** in the release panel is where you pick the Experience site that serves the
link. (CAPTCHA is configured org-wide on the **Setup** tab, not per survey.)

<Warning>
  **A public survey that isn't fully set up fails for the respondent, not for you.** You are logged
  in, so the link works when you test it. Use the **Public link** row's readiness checks, or open
  the link in a private window, before you send it to anyone.
</Warning>

## Nudging people who haven't answered

For surveys sent to people inside your org, the **Follow-up reminders** line on the Get responses
surface opens a **reminder schedule** — one per suite, with its own cadence, audience and copy. You
can preview exactly who is due right now before anything sends.

<Warning>
  **Reminders don't actually send until the user the reminder job runs as has the Reminders Live
  permission.** Granting it to anyone else changes nothing — the check is on the scheduled job's
  running user. Without it the schedule still works and **Send now** still runs — but it only writes
  a dry-run record and emails nobody. The panel shows a banner whenever live sending is off. If
  reminders "aren't arriving", check that permission first. See
  [Installation & Permissions](/admin/installation-and-permissions).
</Warning>

## Who can stop and come back

Whether a respondent who leaves mid-survey can pick up where they were depends on how they arrived:

* **Signed in to your org or site** — yes. Coming back to the survey finds their in-progress draft
  and offers to continue rather than restarting.
* **Personal invitation link** — yes. The invite link itself carries what's needed to resume, so
  reopening the same link continues where they left off.
* **Anonymous, via the public link** — no. The runner has no way to recognise a returning guest: a
  fresh visit to the public link always starts a new draft, and nothing is saved on the
  respondent's device. Anonymous respondents should finish in one sitting.

<Note>
  Worth knowing when you read your completion numbers: a partly-finished response already exists as a
  submission, so an in-progress count is people mid-survey, not people who failed to start.
</Note>

## Verifying a release

1. The **Current release** row shows your new release, with today's date.
2. Open the live link — in a private window if the survey is public.
3. Submit one test response.
4. Confirm the record on the Response Object.

## Releasing again after changes

Edit the sheet, import, and the panel shows **Unpublished changes** again. Release when you're ready.
The previously live release stays as history.

<Note>
  Releasing again on a survey that already has responses is safe. Existing responses keep the fields
  they were collected with; a release only ever adds reporting fields, never removes them.
</Note>
