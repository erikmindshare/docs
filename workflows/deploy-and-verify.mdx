---
title: "Release & Share"
description: "What releasing actually does, why your survey goes live before reporting setup finishes, and how to share the link with people who have no Salesforce login."
---


Importing a survey never makes it live. **Releasing** does — and it takes effect immediately.

Open your survey from the **Surveys** tab. The survey header shows its state: **Draft** before the
first release, then **vN · Accepting responses** (or **Not accepting responses**, **Paused** or
**Closed**). When the draft has changes that are not live, the header shows a **Release** button. It
reads **Release v1** before the first release, then **Release 1 change** or **Release N changes**.

## Step 1 — See what will change

Click the **Release** button. The **Release version N** dialog opens. It lists what this release
does, in three groups:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **For respondents** | What changes for respondents — for example questions and screens that are added, changed or removed |
| **In the survey** | Changes to the survey's own settings |
| **In Salesforce** | What the release changes in your org — for example *This release adds N reporting fields*, or *No new reporting fields needed* |

A survey with more than one form also shows **Forms in this release**, with each form's version now
and after the release.

**Try draft** opens the draft in a new tab, so you can answer it before anyone else sees it.
**Keep editing** closes the dialog and changes nothing.

<Tip>
  If the new-field count looks higher than you expect, check the Questions tab for accidental extra
  rows before you release — each question row mints exactly one reporting field, so stray rows
  inflate the count. That number is the cheapest early warning the dialog gives you.
</Tip>

## Step 2 — The reporting object

AutoSurvey always saves every answer. The **reporting object** is an extra copy for Salesforce
reports: each completed survey creates a record on it, and each question gets a reporting field.

| What **In Salesforce** says | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| *This release creates the reporting object.* | Your first release creates it |
| *No new reporting fields needed.* | Nothing changes in the org |
| *This release adds N reporting fields.* | This release adds N fields |

The **Data** card on the survey's **Settings** tab shows the reporting object. Its name is set when
the survey is created and cannot change. You can turn the reporting object off and on there.

## Step 3 — Release

When the release changes your org, tick the **I approve …** box. It names the org changes that this
release makes. Then click
**Release version N** (or **Release version N to M forms**). The new version is live immediately.

The dialog then shows **Version N is released**, and a toast confirms it — on a first release:
*The survey is accepting responses.* The success view lists the **Ways to reach respondents**: the
internal link, the public link and email invitations, each with its next action. **Try version N**
opens the live version, **Go to Send** opens the Send tab, and **Done** closes the dialog. If the
survey is closed, the release still happens and collection stays closed.

<Note>
  **Going live never waits on reporting setup.** The release is live the moment you click the
  release button; provisioning the new reporting fields happens in the background afterwards,
  usually within a minute. Respondents can start answering straight away.
</Note>

### If reporting setup needs attention

Occasionally the background field setup does not finish first time. The dialog then warns, for
example: *"Reporting setup failed. Version N is released."* The survey header shows **Reporting
needs repair** until it is fixed.

The important part: **the survey stays live and every response is still captured.** AutoSurvey saves
each response; some answers do not show in reports until you repair reporting. **Repair reporting…**
(in the survey's **⋯** menu, or the **Repair reporting** button in the warning) opens the **Repair
reporting** dialog. It is safe to run as many times as you like; it re-checks what is needed
and starts provisioning again.

## Step 4 — Share it

Go to the survey's **Send** tab. **Who can respond** says who can answer now, for example *Now:
anyone with the public link can respond.* Before the first release it says *Nobody yet*.

Under **All channels**, the **Public link** row gives you the link: a **Copy link** button, a **QR ▾** menu with **Download
PNG** and **Copy image**, and a **Start at** picklist that makes the link open at a chosen question.
**Pause public link…** stops new visits through the link until you resume it. The **In-app** row
gives the internal link for people who sign in to Salesforce, and shows how many people have access.
**＋ New send ▾** opens **Email people…**, **Email a campaign…** and **Assign in-app…**.

### If respondents don't have a Salesforce login

Most surveys go to people outside your org. The **Public link** row's readiness checks cover
everything a public link needs — an Experience site bound, the runner page reachable on it, guest
access granted, and this survey serving a live release — and **What’s missing** tells you which
piece is not ready. **Public link setup…** in the release-state menu of the survey header is where
you pick the Experience site that serves the link. (CAPTCHA is configured org-wide on the **Setup**
tab, not per survey.)

<Warning>
  **A public survey that isn't fully set up fails for the respondent, not for you.** You are logged
  in, so the link works when you test it. Use the **Public link** row's readiness checks, or use
  **Try it ▾ → As a guest on your site**, before you send it to anyone.
</Warning>

## Nudging people who haven't answered

For surveys sent to people inside your org, the **Follow-up reminders:** line on the Send tab
shows the reminder schedule — one per survey, with its own cadence, audience and copy.
**Edit schedule…** opens it, and **Turn off** stops it. You can preview exactly who is due right now
before anything sends.

<Warning>
  **Reminders don't actually send until the user the reminder job runs as has the Reminders Live
  permission.** Granting it to anyone else changes nothing — the check is on the scheduled job's
  running user. Without it the schedule still works and **Send now** still runs — but it only writes
  a dry-run record and emails nobody. The reminders line shows **Dry-run mode** whenever live
  sending is off. If
  reminders "aren't arriving", check that permission first. See
  [Installation & Permissions](/admin/installation-and-permissions).
</Warning>

## Who can stop and come back

Whether a respondent who leaves mid-survey can pick up where they were depends on how they arrived:

* **Signed in to your org or site** — yes. Coming back to the survey finds their in-progress draft
  and offers to continue rather than restarting.
* **Personal invitation link** — yes. The invite link itself carries what's needed to resume, so
  reopening the same link continues where they left off.
* **Anonymous, via the public link** — no. The runner has no way to recognise a returning guest: a
  fresh visit to the public link always starts a new draft, and nothing is saved on the
  respondent's device. Anonymous respondents should finish in one sitting.

<Note>
  Worth knowing when you read your completion numbers: a partly-finished response already exists as a
  submission, so an in-progress count is people mid-survey, not people who failed to start.
</Note>

## Verifying a release

1. The survey header shows **vN · Accepting responses** with the new version number.
2. **Release history** (in the survey's **⋯** menu, or the release-state menu) lists the new
   version.
3. Open **Try it ▾** and pick **As yourself** or **As a guest on your site**. Test runs are not
   counted in results.
4. For a real check, open the live link in a private window and submit one response.
5. Confirm the record on the reporting object.

## Releasing again after changes

Edit the questions or the design, or import from the sheet. On the **Questions** tab, a question that
is not in the live release yet shows **New**, and a question that differs from it shows **Edited**.
**Undo** on a row restores the released version of that question, and **Revert all changes… (N)**
drops all draft changes. The header shows **Release N changes** again. Release when you are ready.
Earlier versions stay in **Release history**.

## Stopping collection

**Stop accepting responses…** (in the release-state menu or the **⋯** menu) closes the survey. The
header then shows **Closed**, and **Reopen collection…** opens it again. AutoSurvey does not retire a
survey from the app; a closed survey can always reopen.

<Note>
  Releasing again on a survey that already has responses is safe. Existing responses keep the fields
  they were collected with; a release only ever adds reporting fields, never removes them.
</Note>
