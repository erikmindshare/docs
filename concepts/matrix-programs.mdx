---
title: "Matrix Programs (Audience × Wave)"
description: "Run the same question set across several audiences and several rounds without maintaining a separate survey for each combination."
---


Longitudinal and multi-audience research has the same shape everywhere: one bank of questions, asked
of several groups, several times. **Members** and **supervisors**. **Baseline**, **month three**,
**month twelve**. Twelve variants of nearly the same survey.

Maintaining those as twelve separate surveys is where survey programmes go wrong — a wording fix
lands in nine of them, and by wave three you can no longer compare anything.

A **matrix program** keeps one question bank and marks which questions belong to which
audience × wave combination.

## How it works

Two tabs, working together — once the [Config tab](/workflows/sheets-to-flow#the-config-tab)
switches matrix mode on. Set `Has Audiences` or `Has Rounds` to `TRUE` there. Sheets exported by
AutoSurvey set these keys for you; in a workbook you build by hand you must add them yourself —
without them, the import ignores the matrix silently and builds a single form from the `Active`
column instead.

### 1. The Audience Round Matrix tab

Rows are your audiences, columns are your rounds, and each cell says whether that combination exists
at all:

| Audience \ Round | R1 | R2 | R3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Member | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` |
| Supervisor | `FALSE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` |

Here supervisors join at round two — there is no Supervisor R1 survey, and never will be.

### 2. Per-combination columns on the Questions tab

Every `TRUE` cell above gives you one extra column on the **Questions** tab, named
`<Audience> <Round>`. Mark each question `TRUE` in the combinations it belongs to:

| API Name | Question | Display Type | Member R1 | Member R2 | Supervisor R2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Goal__c` | What is your main goal? | `Text Area` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `FALSE` |
| `Progress__c` | How much progress since we last spoke? | `Slider` | `FALSE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` |
| `Support__c` | How well supported do you feel? | `Likert5` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` |

`Goal__c` is asked of members in both rounds and never of supervisors. `Progress__c` only makes
sense from round two, since there is nothing to compare against at baseline.

<Note>
  Underscores work too — `Member_R2` and `Member R2` are the same column. Use whichever reads better
  in your sheet; audience and round names with spaces in them (`Wave 1`) are fine.
</Note>

## Why this beats separate surveys

* **One place to fix a question.** Reword `Support__c` once and every combination that uses it
  changes together.
* **The same field, across every wave.** Because it is one question with one API Name, all its
  answers land in the same reporting field — so a trend across three rounds is a report, not a data
  project.
* **Adding a wave is a column.** Not a new survey to build and keep in step.

## Naming

The combination column header must match the audience and round names exactly as written in the
matrix. Beyond that, two habits are worth having:

* **Name rounds for what they are, not when they are** — `R1` / `Baseline` / `Month3` outlive
  `Jan2026`, which is wrong the moment the programme slips.
* **Keep audience names short.** They become part of every combination column header, and a wide
  Questions tab is hard to read.

## Getting started

Flip the switch first: on the **Config** tab, set `Has Audiences` or `Has Rounds` to `TRUE` — in a
hand-built workbook, a matrix without that key is silently ignored.

Then start with the audiences and rounds you are sure of. The matrix is not a commitment — adding a round
later is a new column in the matrix and a new column on the Questions tab, and existing responses
are unaffected.

<Warning>
  **An older workbook may have an "Assignments" tab.** It is no longer read. If audience targeting
  is not taking effect, check that the Config tab sets `Has Audiences` or `Has Rounds` to `TRUE`,
  and that you are using the Audience Round Matrix and its per-combination columns — a populated
  Assignments tab is ignored silently, with no warning, and every question then goes to every
  combination.
</Warning>
