Many survey programs have the same shape. One bank of questions goes to several groups, and it goes
to them several times. **Interns** and **supervisors**. **Baseline**, **month three**, **month
twelve**. Without audiences and waves, that is six or twelve copies of nearly the same survey.

Copies are where survey programs go wrong. A wording fix goes into nine of the twelve copies, and by
the third wave you cannot compare the answers.

With audiences and waves, you keep **one survey** with **one bank of questions**. You mark which
questions go to which audience in which wave. AutoSurvey builds one form for each combination.

## The words

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Audience** | A group that answers. | Interns and supervisors. Customers and staff. |
| **Wave** | A point in time when you ask. The Google Sheet calls a wave a **round**. | W1, W2 and W3. Before and after. |
| **Form** | One audience at one wave. Each form has its own link, its own questions and its own results. | The Supervisor form for W2. |
| **Grid** (or matrix) | All audiences against all waves. Each cell is one possible form. A cell can have no form. | 2 audiences × 3 waves = 6 cells. |
| **Survey suite** | A survey that holds several forms. | The Internship Program, with 5 forms. |

A plain survey is a survey with one form. When you add an audience or a wave to it, it becomes a
suite.

## Turn on survey suites

Audiences and waves are off in a new org. An AutoSurvey admin turns them on for the whole org. An
admin is a user who can open **Setup Home**.

There are two ways to turn them on:

* **Setup Home.** Open **Setup Home**, expand **Optional Capabilities**, and turn on **Show survey
  suites**.
* **The note in Survey Suites.** When you have two live surveys, and your first survey is at least
  three days old, **Survey Suites** shows the note "Running the same survey for another group?" one
  time. Click **Tell me more** to open the explainer. An admin sees **Turn on for this org**. Other
  users see where an admin turns it on, and a link to this page.

When suites are on:

* The app says **suite** for a survey that holds several forms.
* The survey's ⋯ menu gets **Manage audiences & waves…**.
* The samples that use audiences and waves become available in **New Survey**.

When suites are on, these do **not** change:

* Your existing surveys, their links and their results.
* A survey gets forms only when you add an audience or a wave to it.

Open tabs show the new words after a reload. To turn suites off, turn off **Show survey suites** on
Setup Home. A survey that already has several forms keeps its forms and its **Manage audiences &
waves…** menu item.

## Try the Internship Program sample

The fastest way to learn audiences and waves is to build the sample and look at it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on survey suites">
    Do this once for the org. See [Turn on survey suites](#turn-on-survey-suites).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the sample">
    In **Survey Suites**, click **New Survey**, then **Start from a sample**. In the **How AutoSurvey
    works** group, pick **Internship Program**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create it">
    Review the survey, then click **Create**. AutoSurvey builds the questions and the five forms.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look at the forms">
    Open the survey. Use the ⋯ menu, **Manage audiences & waves…**, to see the grid and which
    questions each form asks.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The sample is an internship program at a coffee roaster. Interns answer in all three waves.
Supervisors answer from the second wave, when they know their intern:

| Audience \ Wave | W1 | W2 | W3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intern** | Form | Form | Form |
| **Supervisor** | *No form* | Form | Form |

That is 5 forms from one bank of 13 questions. This table shows which forms ask each question:

| Question | Forms that ask it |
| --- | --- |
| Intro text, and "Which site are you based at?" | All 5 |
| Confidence intro, and 3 confidence ratings (espresso, roast, busy counter) | All 5 |
| "How ready do you feel for unsupervised shifts?" | Intern W1, W2, W3 |
| "How ready is your intern for unsupervised shifts?" | Supervisor W2, W3 |
| "What support does this intern need most next?" | Supervisor W2, W3 |
| "What do you most want to get out of this programme?" | Intern W1 |
| "What has changed for you since the last round?" | Intern W2, W3 and Supervisor W2, W3 |
| "I would recommend this programme to someone else." and "Would you take part in another round?" | Intern W3 and Supervisor W3 |

So each form has a different number of rows:

| Form | Rows |
| --- | --- |
| Intern W1 | 8 |
| Intern W2 | 8 |
| Intern W3 | 10 |
| Supervisor W2 | 9 |
| Supervisor W3 | 11 |

Each count includes 2 text rows, the intro and the confidence intro. They show text and ask nothing.

Look for these things in the sample:

* **The same question, the same field.** The three confidence ratings use the same words on every
  form. Their answers go into one field each, so you can compare W1 with W3 in one report.
* **A pair of questions, one for each audience.** Interns rate their own readiness. Supervisors rate
  their intern's readiness. These are two questions, because the words are different. Put them side
  by side in a report to see where interns and supervisors disagree.
* **A question for one wave only.** The goal question is asked only at W1. The recommend and
  continue questions are asked only at W3, at the end of the program.
* **A cell with no form.** Supervisor W1 has no form, because a supervisor does not know the intern
  yet. A cell with no form has no link and no results.

## Manage audiences and waves in the app

Open the survey, then the ⋯ menu, then **Manage audiences & waves…**. The window has two views.

### The Audiences & waves view

This view shows the audiences, the waves and the grid.

* **Add, rename and remove** audiences and waves.
* **Create** a form in an empty cell. The new form starts with the questions of an existing form.
  Then change its questions in the other view. **Create all** creates a form in every empty cell.
* **Disable** a form to stop its public link. Its responses stay, and **Restore** starts the link
  again.
* **Delete** a form only when it has no responses. The window does not offer **Delete** for a form
  that has responses.

Each cell shows the number of questions and the number of responses on that form.

### The Questions & forms view

This view shows every question against every form. Turn a cell on to put the question on that form.
You can turn a question on for all forms, or a form on for all questions. Nothing changes until you
click **Apply** (the button shows the number of changes). **Revert** discards the changes.

<Note>
  The **content** of a question is the same on every form that asks it. You choose **which** forms
  ask a question, not a different wording for each form. If one audience needs different words, make
  a second question, as the sample does for readiness.
</Note>

### Limits

* The survey needs a connected Google Sheet. The window writes the grid and the question choices to
  the sheet. Without a sheet, the window tells you to connect one first.
* A grid can have up to **25** cells, for example 5 audiences × 5 waves. Above **9** cells, the
  window shows a warning, because each cell is a separate form to publish and analyze.
* You cannot split a survey into audiences and waves after it starts to collect responses. To run
  different audiences or waves, create a new suite and set them up there.

## Audiences and waves in the Google Sheet

The window above writes to the survey's Google Sheet. You can also edit the sheet directly. Two tabs
work together, after the [Config tab](/workflows/sheets-to-flow#the-config-tab) turns on matrix
mode.

On the **Config** tab, set `Has Audiences` or `Has Rounds` to `TRUE`. Sheets that AutoSurvey exports
set these keys for you. In a workbook that you build by hand, you must add them yourself. Without
them, the import ignores the matrix and builds a single form from the `Active` column.

### 1. The Audience Round Matrix tab

Rows are your audiences. Columns are your waves (rounds). Each cell says whether that combination
has a form:

| Audience \ Round | W1 | W2 | W3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intern | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` |
| Supervisor | `FALSE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` |

This is the grid of the Internship Program sample. Supervisors join at W2, so there is no
Supervisor W1 form.

### 2. Per-combination columns on the Questions tab

Every `TRUE` cell above gives you one extra column on the **Questions** tab, named
`<Audience> <Round>`. Mark each question `TRUE` in the combinations it belongs to:

| API Name | Question | Intern W1 | Intern W2 | Intern W3 | Supervisor W2 | Supervisor W3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Mem_Conf_Brew__c` | Pull an espresso to the shop standard. | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` |
| `Mem_Self_Ready__c` | How ready do you feel for unsupervised shifts? | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `TRUE` | `FALSE` | `FALSE` |
| `Mem_Goal__c` | What do you most want to get out of this programme? | `TRUE` | `FALSE` | `FALSE` | `FALSE` | `FALSE` |
| `Mem_Recommend__c` | I would recommend this programme to someone else. | `FALSE` | `FALSE` | `TRUE` | `FALSE` | `TRUE` |

<Note>
  Underscores work too. `Intern_W2` and `Intern W2` are the same column. Audience and round names
  with spaces in them (`Wave 1`) are fine.
</Note>

## Compare audiences and waves in reports

Each response records its audience and its wave. The **Survey Submission** record has an
**Audience** field and a **Wave** field, so you can group or filter any report by them.

* **Compare waves.** A question that several forms ask has one API Name, so all its answers go into
  one field. Group that field by **Wave** to see the trend from W1 to W3.
* **Compare audiences.** Put a pair of questions, such as the two readiness questions, side by side,
  and group by **Audience**.
* **Questions that change between waves.** Use the **Survey Answer** object. It has one row per
  answer, so a question set that changes between waves does not change the shape of the data. See
  [Data & Reporting](/concepts/data-and-reporting).

## Why this is better than separate surveys

* **One place to fix a question.** Change the words of a question once, and every form that asks it
  changes together.
* **The same field in every wave.** One question has one API Name, so a trend across three waves is
  a report and not a data project.
* **A new wave is a column.** You do not build a new survey and keep it in step with the others.

## Naming

The combination column header must match the audience and round names in the matrix exactly. Two
more rules help:

* **Name a wave for what it is, not for when it is.** `W1`, `Baseline` and `Month3` stay correct.
  `Jan2026` becomes wrong when the program moves.
* **Keep audience names short.** They are part of every combination column header, and a wide
  Questions tab is hard to read.

## Getting started

1. Turn on survey suites. See [Turn on survey suites](#turn-on-survey-suites).
2. Build the [Internship Program sample](#try-the-internship-program-sample) and look at it.
3. For your own program, start with the audiences and waves that you are sure of. You can add a wave
   later. It is a new column in the matrix and a new column on the Questions tab, and it does not
   change existing responses.
