Every survey has a **design**: its colours, type, spacing, how options are framed, and your brand.
You edit it on the survey's **Design** tab, and you can start from something rather than from
nothing.

When you create a survey, the **Look** section of the **Review** step lets you pick a theme, then
its palette. A sample question shows the colours, the type and the corners of your choice. The step is optional: the standard look applies when you choose nothing, and you can change
it at any time on the Design tab. A new survey starts with its title and intro centred over
left-aligned questions.

## The one thing to understand first

<Note>
  **Adopting a theme or a palette is a copy, not a link.** Picking one writes its values into *your*
  survey's design and then has no further hold on it. Your design keeps a note of which theme it
  came from, but that is a record, not a connection — nothing flows through it later.
</Note>

Two consequences, and they run in opposite directions:

* **Nothing changes under you.** A theme edited later — by you or by an update — cannot alter a
  survey that already adopted it. What you released stays what you released.
* **Improvements don't reach you either.** If a built-in theme is refined, surveys that already took
  its values keep the old ones. Re-adopt it if you want the change.

It also means a survey *shows* as using a theme only while its values still match that theme
exactly. Change one colour and it becomes your own design — which is correct, not a bug.

## Themes

Nineteen themes ship, and they differ in structure as much as colour — how questions are framed,
how much air there is, how the page is anchored:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Card-based** | Clean Card, Soft Cards, Open Cards, Signature Tint |
| **Editorial** | Editorial, Warm Editorial, Boxed Serif |
| **Spacious** | Easy Read, Conversational, Full Bleed, Edge to Edge |
| **Compact** | Dense, Field Grid, Plain Rhythm |
| **Other** | Bold Modern, Clean Neutral, Night, Washed, Boxed |

Adopt one from a survey's **Design** tab. The **Theme Library** tab is a different thing: it is the
org-level shelf. It shows the themes and palettes **In this org** and those **Available from
AutoSurvey**, and **⟳ Get all latest** pulls the latest in one click. On the Design tab,
**↻ Refresh themes** fetches the latest themes and palettes and does not touch your own saved
designs. **The Library stocks the shelf; the Design tab styles a survey.**

<Tip>
  Themes carry structure; palettes carry colour. Adopt a theme first for the shape you want, then
  change the colour underneath it — that ordering saves the most work.
</Tip>

## Palettes

Seventeen palettes set the colour slots in one move — accents, surfaces, the ground the survey sits
on. They include ramps (`rampSky`, `rampTeal`, `rampAmber`, `rampGold`, `rampRust`, `rampViolet`),
named schemes (`ocean`, `lagoon`, `fern`, `ember`, `iris`, `slate`, `midnight`, `dusk`, `harbour`),
and two tuned to look like the tools people arrive from (`googleForms`, `surveyMonkey`).

Like a theme, a palette is a shortcut — it writes the values and lets go.

<Note>
  Palette colours are **measured, not chosen**: each was checked for contrast so text stays legible
  on the surface behind it. If you fine-tune a colour by hand afterwards, that guarantee is yours to
  keep.
</Note>

## Your brand

Set your **logo** and **favicon** on the Design tab under **Header & text** — logo size and
alignment sit alongside them — and your **background image** under **Colour**.

To centre the survey title, the subtitle and the intro text while the questions stay on the left,
use **Title and intro alignment** under **Layout**. **Same as questions** follows the question
alignment.

## Welcome and ending screens

The Design tab also holds the copy a respondent sees before they start and after they finish. Both
are edited in place, with a preview.

The survey title, welcome, header, footer and ending text belong to the survey, not to the theme.
They carry a **Survey setting** label, adopting a theme never changes them, and each has its own
Save button. The **Help text style** control also shows the survey's own setting with the
**Survey setting** label.

## Trying things safely

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Respondent preview** | The right side of the Design tab shows your draft as a respondent sees it, one screen at a time. Answers you type there are not saved. If your draft has changes that the preview does not show yet, **Update preview** brings them in |
| **Save draft** | Saves the theme, layout and style to the draft. **Release** publishes them to respondents |
| **Restore your settings** | Put everything back to how it stood before you started trying themes this session |
| **Share this design** | Take a design out — **Download .json** or **Copy** — then **Apply this design** on another survey |
| **Save theme** | Name a design you've built and it appears in your themes, ready to apply to any other survey |

<Note>
  **The preview shows the draft.** Its heading reads **Respondent preview — draft**, with the live
  version number when one exists. Before the first release, a **Not published yet** note sits above
  the preview: the preview shows what a respondent will see, and a release is what makes it
  reachable.
</Note>

If you switch to another survey or leave the tab with unsaved design changes, the Design tab asks
first. You can save, discard, or keep editing.

<Tip>
  **Restore is the reason to experiment freely.** It reverses a run of theme-flipping in one click —
  so try five themes rather than agonising over the first.
</Tip>
